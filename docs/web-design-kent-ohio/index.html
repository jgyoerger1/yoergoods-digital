What the site has to do

What a Kent contractor needs from a website

A homeowner sizing up a contractor online asks three things: can I see the work, do you do my kind of job, and how do I reach you. These five parts answer them, and every one is on the Yoerger Renovations site.

So the job gets found

A page for each service

Homeowners search for the job, not the company: a kitchen remodel, a new deck, a finished basement. The Kent site has six service pages: kitchens, bathrooms, basements, decks, living rooms and handyman work. Every one is written around what local homeowners search for, with its own questions and answers and a photo from a real job at the top.

So the work gets seen

A gallery the owner updates himself

Finished work is the sales pitch, and it goes stale if adding a photo means calling the web designer. Ryan drops phone photos into a folder and names each one with a caption. The site turns those folders into resized images, a page for each project, filters and alt text. It launched with fifteen projects.

So the quote starts sooner

A quote form that takes photos

A quote for a bathroom starts with seeing the bathroom. Homeowners attach pictures of the room to the request. The photos are shrunk in the browser and emailed to the owner with the details, so he can answer from his inbox with the conversation already started. Spam is kept out without making real customers solve a CAPTCHA puzzle.

So a phone can reach you

Call or text, one tap away

Remodeling customers browse on their phones, so the call-or-text button on the Kent site is always one tap away on a phone. Phone photos get the same care: each one is resized twice, large and small, and its orientation is fixed automatically, so a sideways shot never shows up sideways.

So nothing renews every month

No monthly platform bill

The Kent site is plain static pages on free hosting behind the business's own domain, yoergerrenovations.com. There is no platform subscription, no plugin to update and nothing to break. It also ships with local business structured data, a sitemap, and a clean title and description on every page, so a search engine can tell what the business does and where.
