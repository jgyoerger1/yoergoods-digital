For sites that haven't changed in a while

Your site isn't broken. It's just stuck in 2019.

Slow on phones, old photos, a layout that squints on a small screen, and a "news" page that stopped in a different decade. You know. You've been putting it off because you expect the fix to cost a fortune. It doesn't have to.

Five signs it's time

If two of these are true, a refresh pays for itself.

01

It's slow or awkward on a phone

More than half of your visitors are on one. If they pinch and zoom, they leave, and Google notices too.

02

The photos and prices are old

Customers assume the business is as out of date as the site. A refresh swaps in current work and current details.

03

You're embarrassed to send the link

If you'd rather text someone a photo than your own URL, the site has stopped doing its job.

04

Nobody knows how to update it

The person who built it is gone, the login is lost, the platform wants a monthly fee. Time to own it again.

What a refresh keeps

The things that already work.

Your pages, your words where they're still true, your domain, and your Google history. Every old address redirects to its new home so nobody lands on a dead page and search rankings carry over instead of starting from zero.

What a refresh fixes

The things that make people leave.

A new design that works on every screen, load times measured in a blink, current photos and details, a contact form that actually reaches you, and the technical plumbing (titles, descriptions, structured data, sitemap) that helps Google understand you again.

How a refresh runs

Managed like a project, because the risk is in the handover, not the design.

A refresh touches a live site that customers are using right now. That's exactly the kind of work a project manager is for: inventory what exists, plan the change, make the switch cleanly, confirm nothing broke.

  1. AuditEvery page, link, photo and form on the current site, listed. What stays, what goes, what's missing.
  2. PlanFixed price, dates and a redirect map, so old links keep working after the switch.
  3. RedesignHomepage first for approval, then the rest. The old site stays live the whole time.
  4. SwitchOne clean cutover, usually off-hours. Forms tested, redirects checked, Google told about the changes.
  5. WatchThirty days of fixes included, and a check that search traffic carried over.
