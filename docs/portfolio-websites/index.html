Stand out in a stack of resumes.

A resume says what you did. A portfolio site shows it: the project, the before and after, the numbers, the way you think. One fast page on your own domain that a recruiter can open from your resume, your LinkedIn or a QR code, and remember.

The problem with a PDF

Everyone's resume looks the same, and most never get read past the top third.

Applicant tracking systems flatten your work into keywords. The human who finally opens it skims for a few seconds. There is no room for the project you're proudest of, the slide that landed the budget, or the spreadsheet model nobody else could build.

What a portfolio site changes

You pick what they see first, and you show instead of claim.

A clear headline about who you are and what you're good at, four to six pieces of real work with a sentence on the result, a short story, your credentials, and a resume they can download. Linked everywhere your name appears, it turns "sounds qualified" into "I want to talk to this person."

What goes on it

Six parts. One page. Nothing to maintain.

01

A headline that frames you

Who you are, what you're best at, who you help. The sentence a recruiter repeats to the hiring manager.

02

Four to six pieces of work

Projects, deliverables, decks, models, code, designs. Each with the situation, what you did and what changed, in three lines.

03

Your story and credentials

Career in a paragraph, degrees and certifications as clean badges, a photo that looks like you on a good day.

04

Resume, contact, proof

One-click resume download, a contact section that works on a phone, LinkedIn connected, and your own domain so the link is yours for life.
